## Description
Grandma swears a candy bar cost 35 cents. Today it's $2.29. What happened?! Finn (14, still sharing his piggy bank with a moth) hunts down the villain behind rising prices: Inflato, a very smug talking balloon. With help from Grandma Ruth and Doug from the corner store, you'll learn what inflation is, why your cash slowly "melts", and how to fight back.

You'll learn: what inflation and the CPI are, the Rule of 72, why prices rise (too much money, higher costs, money printing), the 2022 inflation spike, shrinkflation and unit prices, hyperinflation (Germany 1923, Zimbabwe's 100-trillion-dollar bill), how central banks use interest rates, why a raise can still make you poorer, and a simple 5-step anti-inflation plan.

Numbers used: $100 at 3% inflation for 10 years buys about $74 of today's stuff; Rule of 72 (3% → prices double in ~24 years, 2% → ~36). US prices are about 3× higher than in 1985 (CPI), so 35¢ then ≈ $1.05 today. US federal minimum wage: $3.35/hour in 1985, $7.25 today. US inflation peaked at 9.1% in June 2022, the highest in about 40 years. The Fed and the Bank of Canada aim for about 2% a year. Zimbabwe issued a 100-trillion-dollar note in 2009. Investing is never guaranteed: stocks have usually beaten inflation over long periods, but not every year. Sources: US Bureau of Labor Statistics (CPI), US Department of Labor, Federal Reserve, Bank of Canada.

## Description
Canada gives you three money cheat codes – the TFSA, the RRSP and the FHSA – and most people never press the buttons. Finn (14, now in a toque, piggy bank still a moth hotel) explains them all, with help from Grandma Ruth, Bernie the Beaver, and Gordon the Tax Moose, who would really prefer you didn't watch this.

You'll learn: how the TFSA works (and the #1 mistake: leaving it in cash), 2 TFSA traps, why an emergency fund comes first, how the RRSP refund works, the FHSA for first-time home buyers, how $7,000 a year could grow to about $1.4 million, why fees matter more than you think, why high-interest debt goes first, and a simple 6-step game plan.

Numbers used (2026): TFSA limit $7,000; cumulative room $109,000 if you were 18+ and resident since 2009 and never contributed; over-contribution penalty 1% per month. RRSP limit 18% of last year's earned income up to $33,810. FHSA $8,000 a year, $40,000 lifetime. Typical Canadian credit card rate ≈ 20%. Examples assume 7% a year, which is NOT guaranteed: $7,000/yr from 25 to 65 ≈ $1.4M (you put in $280K); from 35 ≈ $661K; $100/month for 40 years ≈ $262K; $100K for 30 years with a 2% fee ≈ $432K vs a 0.25% fee ≈ $710K. Sources: Canada Revenue Agency (TFSA, RRSP and FHSA pages).

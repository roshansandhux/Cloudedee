Numbers used (US, 2025–26): the average new-car payment is around $750/month, and about 1 in 5 new-car buyers pay $1,000+ (Edmunds). Typical APRs: new ≈ 7%, used ≈ 11%, bad credit 20%+ (Edmunds, Experian). A $40,000 loan at 7%: 4 years ≈ $958/mo and $5,977 interest; 6 years ≈ $682/mo and $9,101; 7 years ≈ $604/mo and $10,711. A $20,000 used-car loan over 6 years: 11% ≈ $7,409 interest, 21% ≈ $15,332. New cars often lose about 20% of their value in year one and about half within ~5 years. About 1 in 4 trade-ins carry negative equity (Edmunds). AAA estimates a new car costs about $11–12k a year to own, all-in. $750/month invested at ~7% a year ≈ $130k after 10 years and ≈ $1.97M after 40 years. 7% is an assumption, not a guarantee: investing can lose money. 20/4/10 is a rule of thumb, not a law.
